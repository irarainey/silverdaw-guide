# Introduction

Have a song you'd like to reshape, or a vocal you want to put over a different
beat? **Silverdaw** gives you a place to try those ideas. Bring in your audio,
choose the parts you want, and arrange them into a mix of your own.

Silverdaw is a free, open-source Digital Audio Workstation (DAW) for Windows.
You can work with songs, loops, vocals, and samples, or record a voice or
instrument alongside your arrangement.

You don't need studio experience to begin. Start with one audio file and a
short section you like; the [Quick Start](/1.10/guide/quick-start) takes you
through cutting it into loops, adding effects, and saving an audio file.

> This guide targets Silverdaw 1.10.0 and the 1.10.x release series.

## Who Silverdaw is for

Silverdaw is for combining and reshaping audio into remixes and mashups.
Work at your own pace: listen, move a section, try an effect, and listen again.
It is a studio creation tool, not a live-performance instrument.

## The ideas behind it

Three ideas will help you find your way:

- **The Library holds your material.** Imported songs, saved loops, stems, and
  recordings stay here, ready to use in your project.
- **The timeline is your arrangement.** Each piece of audio you place there is
  a *clip*. Move, trim, and repeat clips to build the mix.
- **Your source files stay untouched.** Trimming, tempo, pitch, and effects
  change how clips play, not the original audio. You can try a different sound
  without overwriting the file you started with.

## What you can do

- **Find material for your mix.** [Browse your audio folders](/1.10/guide/files)
  and listen before importing, or reuse stems and samples from another project
  through the [Library](/1.10/guide/library).
- **Make a different arrangement.** [Cut clips into loops](/1.10/guide/clip-editor),
  repeat a phrase, or blend sections together on the
  [timeline](/1.10/guide/timeline). Tempo matching helps parts play in time.
- **Work with just part of a song.** [Separate stems](/1.10/guide/stems) to
  use the vocals, drums, bass, or remaining instruments on their own.
- **Add your own performance.** [Record a voice or instrument](/1.10/guide/recording)
  over the mix, or create a vinyl-style pattern in the
  [Scratch Editor](/1.10/guide/scratch-editor).
- **Shape the sound.** [Balance tracks and add effects](/1.10/guide/mixing),
  draw changes that happen over time, or use installed
  [VST3 effect plugins](/1.10/guide/plugins).
- **Make a file to share.** [Export a mixdown](/1.10/guide/export) when you're
  ready to listen outside Silverdaw.

## How to use this guide

You don't have to read this guide from beginning to end. If Silverdaw is open,
start with the [Quick Start](/1.10/guide/quick-start). If you still need the
application, begin with [Installation](/1.10/guide/installation).

Once you're making sound, follow the pages for the task at hand:
[arrange your clips](/1.10/guide/timeline),
[blend and shape the tracks](/1.10/guide/mixing), or
[record something of your own](/1.10/guide/recording).
The [window tour](/1.10/guide/the-window) helps when you need to find a control.

If terms like *sample rate*, *bit depth*, or *WAV* are new to you,
[Digital Audio Basics](/1.10/guide/digital-audio-basics) explains them in plain
language. Look up a term when you need it; there's no theory to learn before
you can start.
